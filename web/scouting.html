      How the comparison works

      Each player’s rate uses reported events per 90 minutes with a known value. A recorded zero counts. Missing values and their minutes are left out. Real Madrid’s reference pools the players in the same position.

      Opponent places come from completed league matches before the fixture’s date, ordered by points, goal difference and goals scored, once every club has played five games. A tie across sixth place stays unclassified; all opponents includes those games.

      Players are listed by the chosen measure per 90 in their own league, with no league multiplier. Position, minutes, data coverage and transfer cost all matter when reading a gap.

      About these numbers
      • Goals and scorers come from OpenLigaDB, a community feed, for the Champions League, the Premier League and the Bundesliga. It has no current La Liga season, so La Liga goals are not here; La Liga results are on the Table page.
      • A Premier League or Bundesliga club is matched to OpenFootball’s schedule. When the two feeds report different results for a match, it keeps OpenFootball’s result and loses its goal list.
      • The opponent’s place comes from a table of that competition’s results dated before the match, ordered by points, goal difference and goals scored: the Champions League uses its league-phase table. A match is not classified while any club has played fewer than five games, or when the opponent is level on sixth place.
      • The feed has no minutes or appearances, so these are counts of goals, not rates. A goal the feed does not name counts for the club only, and an own goal counts for the club and for no player.
      • Real Madrid in La Liga compares their goals per match with the pooled matches of the other La Liga clubs, from OpenFootball’s reported results.
      • Real Madrid scorers lists every player the Wikipedia article on the season credits with a goal, in all competitions, by goals and then name, as of the article’s last edit.
      • Where the feed lists one player under two forms of his name (“N. Jackson” and “Nicolas Jackson”), his goals are counted once. Different initials are never joined.
